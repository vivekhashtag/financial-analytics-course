You have access to something no previous generation of students had: an infinitely patient tutor that never sleeps. Used well, AI can halve your learning time. Used badly, it makes you feel like you're learning while you learn nothing.

This page is the contract.

## The verify habit — three steps, every time

1. **Ask** AI for the code or explanation.
2. **Interrogate:** "What assumptions does this make? When would this be wrong?"
3. **Verify** on a case where you already know the answer.

That third step has a name in this course: the **tracer bullet**. Test your average-calculator on `[1, 2, 3]` where you know the answer is 2, *before* you point it at 5,000 transactions.

<Callout type="danger">
**The rule that matters most:** you may only run AI-generated code you can explain line by line. You're allowed to use AI to reach that explanation, you're not allowed to skip it.
</Callout>

## AI-usage disclosure

Every exercise and the capstone include a one-line disclosure: *"AI assisted with ___"* or *"AI was not used."*

Not to punish use, to build the professional habit. In a real finance job, undisclosed AI use inside a model is a compliance incident.
