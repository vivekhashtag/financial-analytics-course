<Callout type="warn">
These four appear in **every lab** from Module 9 onward. This is the most important page in Part A.
</Callout>

## 1. Look-ahead bias — "using tomorrow's newspaper"

Using information that was **not available at decision time**.

A backtest "buys stocks with strong annual earnings" using earnings *for* the year, but earnings are announced months *after* year-end. The strategy is time-travelling. Countless published, apparently profitable strategies die on this alone.

> **Always ask:** on the morning of the decision, was this number actually knowable?

## 2. Survivorship bias — "interviewing only the winners"

Failed, delisted and merged entities silently dropped from history.

"The average mutual fund returned 9%!", computed only on funds that still exist. The ones that lost money were shut down and vanished from the database.

In WWII, statistician Abraham Wald was asked where to armour bombers, based on where returning planes had bullet holes. His answer: armour where they *didn't* have holes. The planes hit there never came back to be measured.

> **Always ask:** who is missing from this dataset, and did they leave at random?

## 3. Point-in-time bias — "history, quietly rewritten"

Using restated or revised figures as if they were the original print.

GDP gets revised. Company financials get restated. A model trained on today's *revised* series predicts the past beautifully, because it saw cleaner numbers than anyone actually had at the time. The fix has a name: **point-in-time databases**, which store what was believed *as of each date*.

Your own `company_financials.csv` has this trap planted: MoneyMart's FY20-21 revenue was first reported as ₹3,511 cr and later restated to ₹3,366 cr.

> **Always ask:** is this the number as originally reported, or as later corrected?

## 4. Regime change — "fitting through an earthquake"

Fitting one model across a structural break as though the world never changed.

A deposit-behaviour model trained on 2010–2019 (near-zero rates) deployed into 2022–23 (the fastest hiking cycle in 40 years) sees customers behaving "irrationally." They aren't, the *regime* changed. Indian examples: pre/post-demonetisation 2016, pre/post-COVID.

> **Always ask:** did the world change somewhere inside my data's timespan?
