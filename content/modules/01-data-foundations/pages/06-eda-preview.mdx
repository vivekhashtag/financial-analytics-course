A preview of what Module 3 will formalise as Exploratory Data Analysis. Four habits, all of them done with your eyes before any code.

## 1. Look at the shape

<DistributionCompare />

Daily stock returns against a normal bell curve. Notice the tails: **fat tails are the norm in finance.** An event the bell curve calls "once in 10,000 years" shows up in real markets every few years. This is why Module 10 exists.

## 2. Look for holes

<MissingCalendar dataset="nifty50_prices" />

Weekends and holidays are *expected* gaps. A random missing Tuesday is a *problem* gap. Your NIFTY file has two of the latter, 2023-06-14 and 2024-02-21, and they're invisible unless you check against the trading calendar.

It also has the opposite trap: Diwali **Muhurat trading** sessions, which fall on holidays and look like errors but are real half-sessions. A rule that strips "holiday" rows would delete genuine data. Domain knowledge beats the outlier rule.

## 3. Look for the impossible

Negative volumes. Prices of zero. A transaction dated **1970-01-01**, which almost never means time travel and almost always means a missing timestamp defaulting to the start of Unix time.

## 4. A crisis is not an outlier

<CrisisChart />

Plot 2008 or March 2020 and a statistician's instinct says trim those points. A finance professional knows **those are the observations that matter most.** Cut the crises out of your data and you have built a model that works right up until it's needed.
