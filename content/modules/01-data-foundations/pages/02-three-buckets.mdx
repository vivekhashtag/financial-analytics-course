Every piece of financial data falls into one of three buckets. Knowing which one you're holding tells you how to handle it.

## Bucket 1 · Quantitative — structured, numeric, model-ready

| Type | Examples | In plain English |
|---|---|---|
| Market data | Prices, returns, volumes, order books | What things cost, minute by minute |
| Financial statements | P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, ratios | A company's report card |
| Transactions & ledgers | Payments, trades, positions | The receipts — every single one |
| Macro series | GDP, CPI inflation, repo rate, FX | The economy's vital signs |
| Reference data | Security IDs, ratings, corporate actions | The phonebook that says which stock is which |

## Bucket 2 · Qualitative — unstructured, textual, judgement-laden

Filings narrative (the *story* around the numbers), earnings calls (where **tone** matters), analyst research, credit memos, news, regulatory notices, contracts, KYC documents.

<Callout type="info">
Qualitative data is no longer un-analysable. Modern NLP turns narrative into numbers, tone scores, topic shifts, "how much did the risk-factors section grow this year?" We won't build NLP in this course, but you must know this bucket exists, because much of the **why** lives here.
</Callout>

## Bucket 3 · Alternative — the new frontier

Satellite images of car parks (forecast retail sales *before* earnings) · card-spending panels · web traffic and app downloads · job postings · shipping records.

The alternative-data market grew from roughly $1.6B in 2020 to about $10B in 2024, with projections running much higher. *Projections are exactly the kind of number this module teaches you to treat with suspicion.*
