Three operations answer most business questions:

## groupby — "per segment, per city, per RM…"

Split → apply → combine. And the trick used everywhere: **the mean of a 0/1 flag is a rate.**

<CodePeek code={`clients.groupby("segment").agg(
    clients=("client_id", "count"),
    total_aum=("aum_inr", "sum"),
    churn_rate=("churned", "mean"),   # 0/1 mean = the rate
)`} />

## merge — the join decision

`how=` is not a technicality. It changes your answer:

| how | Keeps | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| `left` | every client | — the honest default here |
| `inner` | only matched rows | **silently drops non-transactors: survivorship bias by keyword** |
| `outer` | everything | for reconciliations |

Notebook 3C demonstrates the trap live: filter to active clients before averaging AUM and "our average client" changes by double digits. Neither number is wrong, presenting the filtered one as the population average is.

## pivot_table — the report grid

<CodePeek code={`df.pivot_table(index="city", columns="segment",
               values="aum_inr", aggfunc="sum")`} />

<Callout type="warn">
Never report a rate without its count. A churn rate of 67% built on 3 clients is noise wearing a percentage. In 3C you build the rate pivot and the count pivot side by side; in Module 3.5 the same rule becomes a HAVING clause.
</Callout>
